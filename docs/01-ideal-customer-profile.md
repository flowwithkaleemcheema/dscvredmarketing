# Dscvred Marketing: Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) v1

> **Status:** v1, built from founder inputs. Items marked *(validate)* are working assumptions. Confirm or correct them in the first 10–20 discovery calls.
> **Launch market:** United States. Australia and the UK follow in phase 2 (see §11).

---

## 1. The ICP in one sentence

**US-based, owner-operated residential remodelers doing $1M–$5M a year in kitchen, bath and whole-home remodels ($40k–$150k+ per project). They depend on referrals and shared-lead platforms (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Houzz, Thumbtack) and want a predictable flow of 2–4 extra signed projects a month, without paying to fight four other contractors over every lead.**

---

## 2. Why this niche

| Founder criterion | How remodelers score |
|---|---|
| **High deal value, so ad budgets pay off** | Average project $40k–$150k+. At ~30–35% gross margin, **one signed kitchen ≈ $15k–$30k gross profit**, which pays for several months of agency fee plus ad spend. |
| **Budget to pay for services** | At $1M–$5M revenue, a $3k ad budget plus a ~$4–5k fee is roughly 2–5% of revenue, the normal range for a growing contractor. Most already pay for leads (Angi, Houzz Pro), so marketing spend is a habit. |
| **They struggle with marketing** | Lead flow depends on referrals and rented platforms. Owners are on job sites, not in ad accounts. Few have tracking, a follow-up system or a conversion-focused website. |
| **Decision makers are easy to reach** | The owner *is* the buyer, the seller and often the person who answers the phone. No front desk or procurement committee in the way. They are publicly listed on Google Maps, Houzz, Angi, state license databases and LinkedIn. |
| **They can close** | Remodeling owners sell in the home every week. Our job is to put qualified homeowners in front of them; they're already good at closing. |

| Attribute | Ideal | Acceptable range |
|---|---|---|
| Country (v1) | United States | AU / UK in phase 2 |
| Business type | Residential remodeler or design-build firm | Kitchen and bath specialist with a showroom |
| Core work | Kitchens, bathrooms, whole-home / interior remodels | Plus basements and additions as secondary work |
| Annual revenue | **$1M–$5M** | $800k–$6M if other signals are strong |
| Average project size | **$40k–$150k+** | Floor of ~$25k |
| Team | 5–25 people (crews, PM, office; subs common) | Owner plus at least one PM or lead carpenter |

| Capacity | Can take on **2–4 more projects a month** | Hiring, or able to add a crew or subs |
| Marketing budget | **≥ $3,000/month ad spend** plus our fee | Hard minimum: $3k ad spend |
| Online reputation | Google Business Profile with **15+ reviews at 4.5★+**, real project photos | 10+ reviews at 4.3★+ |
| Service area | Suburban metro with established housing stock and median home values of **$400k+** | Affluent pockets inside bigger metros |
| Licensing | Licensed and insured where required | Required |

### Disqualifiers (don't take them on)
- Average project below ~$25k (handymen, cosmetic-only, single-trade installers)
- New construction only, commercial only, or insurance or restoration work only
- Rating below 4.0★, or a pattern of unresolved complaints. We can't advertise a bad product.
- Won't call new leads back within the same business day, or won't use the follow-up system
- No capacity and no plan to add any
- A franchise location where corporate controls the marketing
- Wants "leads for $50 each" and treats marketing as a cost, not an investment

## 4. Unit economics: why the offer pays for itself *(validate)*

Planning assumptions for one client in one metro:

| Step | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Ad spend | $3,000–$5,000 / month (Google Search + Local Services Ads + Meta) |
| Cost per qualified lead | ~$100–$250 for kitchen, bath and whole-home searches |
| Qualified leads | ~15–35 / month |
| Lead → booked in-home or virtual consultation | 35–50% (speed-to-lead plus nurture system) |
| Booked consultations | ~6–12 / month |
| Consultation → signed project | 20–35% (owner-led sale) |
| **Signed projects** | **~2–3 / month** |
| Average project value | ~$60k–$75k |
| **Monthly signed revenue** | **~$120k–$225k** |
| Client's total investment (fee + ads) | ~$7k–$10k / month |

**Bottom line:** one signed project covers the whole engagement for months. Every additional project is profit. This math drives the offer, the guarantee and the landing page copy.

## 5. The decision maker

**Who:** The owner (titles: Owner, President, Founder, CEO, Principal). In $3–5M firms, sometimes a GM or sales manager shares the decision. A spouse or co-owner often runs the office and finances and influences the decision.

**Profile *(validate)*:**
- Age 35–60. Came up in the trades (carpenter, then PM, then owner) or as a strong salesperson who started a firm
- Spends days on job sites and runs estimates and design consultations evenings and Saturdays
- Proud of craftsmanship. Hates being treated like a commodity or compared on price
- Sceptical of marketers. Values plain talk, numbers and people who "get" construction
- Good at closing in the home; bad at producing consistent demand
- Checks email on their phone between job sites, and is active in Facebook groups for contractors and remodelers

**What they want:**
1. **Predictable pipeline.** Crews booked 3–6 months out, all year, including the Q1 slow season
2. **Better clients.** Homeowners with real budgets ($50k+) who value quality, not price shoppers
3. **Independence.** Stop paying Angi or HomeAdvisor for shared leads and stop depending on referrals alone
4. **Leverage.** Get out of chasing leads so they can run, and grow, the business
5. **Clarity.** Know what each marketing dollar returns

**What they fear:**
- Wasting money on marketing that sends "clicks" and "impressions" but no signed jobs
- Long contracts with no way out
- Tyre-kickers and $15k budgets for $60k kitchens
- Crews sitting idle, losing good tradespeople, or having to lay people off
- Not knowing whether the slow month is coming

## 6. Pain points in their own words *(validate in discovery calls)*

Use these phrases in ads, cold email and the landing page.

**Referral dependence: "feast or famine"**
- "When referrals are flowing we're slammed. When they dry up, we scramble."
- "I've got no idea what next quarter looks like."
- "January and February kill us every year."

**Shared-lead platforms (Angi / HomeAdvisor / Houzz / Thumbtack)**
- "They sell the same lead to four other contractors."
- "By the time I call, they've already talked to three guys."
- "Half of them are just shopping prices."
- "I'm paying for leads that don't even answer the phone."
- "Their budget is $20k and they want a $70k kitchen."

**Time and systems**
- "I'm on job sites all day. I don't have time to do marketing."
- "Leads come in and sit there because nobody calls them back fast enough."
- "We give estimates and never follow up."
- "I don't know where my jobs actually come from."

**Positioning**
- "People compare us to the cheapest guy on the list."
- "Our work is better, but our website doesn't show it."

## 8. Objections and how we answer them

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "I've tried marketing before. It didn't work." | We report signed projects and revenue, not clicks. There's a guarantee (see offer), and no long lock-in. |
| "Referrals work fine for us." | Referrals are great, and they're out of your control. We build a second stream you can turn up or down, so referrals become a bonus rather than your only source. |
| "Leads from online are junk / price shoppers." | Shared-lead platforms sell price shoppers. We run *your* brand, filter by budget and project type before booking, and the leads are 100% exclusive. |
| "We're busy right now." | Remodel pipelines lag 60–120 days. Today's leads become your Q1 projects. The time to build the pipeline is while you're busy. |
| "It's too expensive." | One signed kitchen ≈ $15k–$30k gross profit, which covers months of fee plus ads. The real cost is an idle crew. |
| "You don't have remodeling case studies." | True, and we're upfront about it. We've produced results in other industries, and we're taking a small number of founding remodeling partners on terms that shift the risk to us. |
| "I don't want a long contract." | (Settled when we build the offer: guarantee and contract terms.) |
| "I don't have time to manage this." | Done for you. Your only job is showing up to booked consultations, and the follow-up system handles the chasing. |

## 9. Buying triggers: when to reach out

High-intent moments that make outreach land:
- **Slow season approaching:** pitching Sept–Nov for Q1 pipeline is the strongest window
- **Rising Angi / HomeAdvisor costs,** or frustration with lead quality
- **Capacity just grew:** hired a PM, added a crew, opened a showroom (job posts are visible signals)
- **Moving upmarket:** raising prices, shifting from baths to full kitchens or whole-home
- **Expanding the service area** or opening a second location
- **Owner wants to step out of sales** or production
- **A big referral source dried up** (e.g. a designer or realtor partner)

## 10. Where to find them and how to reach them

**Primary channels (founder's choice):** cold email + LinkedIn / Facebook DMs.

### List building
| Source | Why it's useful |
|---|---|
| **Google Maps scraping** ("kitchen remodeler", "bathroom remodeling", "design build remodeler", "home remodeling contractor") by metro | Biggest source. Includes review count and rating for qualification. |
| **Houzz Pro directory** (especially badge holders and paid profiles) | They already pay for marketing, a strong budget signal |
| **Angi / HomeAdvisor listed pros** | They already pay for shared leads, so the pain is real and the budget exists |
| **NARI / NKBA member directories** | Established, professional firms |
| **State contractor license databases** | Verifies licensing; good for enrichment |
| **LinkedIn Sales Navigator**: Industry = Construction; titles = Owner, President, Founder, CEO; keywords = remodel, design-build, kitchen, bath; headcount 2–50; US | Finding owners and sending DMs |
| **Facebook groups** for remodelers and contractor business owners | Warm DMs, content, community presence |
| **Apollo / Clay** enrichment | Owner email, phone, LinkedIn URL |

### Lead-scoring signals for outreach lists
| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| GBP 20+ reviews at 4.6★+ | +2 |
| Listed on Houzz Pro / Angi / HomeAdvisor | +2 |
| Portfolio shows kitchens, baths or whole-home work | +2 |
| Hiring (job posts for PM, carpenter or estimator) | +1 |
| Already running Google or Meta ads (Google Ads Transparency Center / Meta Ad Library) | +1 |
| Weak website (no clear CTA, slow, no tracking pixel) | +1 (a hook for personalisation) |
| Projects look small or handyman-type | −3 |
| Rating below 4.0★ | Disqualify |

### Starting metros (US): suburban, affluent, older housing stock *(validate)*
Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, Austin, Atlanta, Charlotte, Raleigh, Nashville, Phoenix, Denver, Tampa, Orlando, Chicago suburbs, Minneapolis–St. Paul, Columbus, Seattle Eastside. One client per service area (exclusivity becomes part of the offer).

## 12. Agency context (feeds the offer)

- **Agency:** Dscvred Marketing
- **Delivery:** Paid ads (Google Search, Local Services Ads, Meta), local SEO and Google Business Profile, landing pages, funnels and CRO, **plus** a speed-to-lead and nurture CRM system (missed-call text-back, instant SMS and email, nurture sequences, old-lead and past-estimate reactivation)
- **Proof:** No remodeling case studies yet. Founder has 10+ years in growth marketing from in-house roles: a Dubai medical-aesthetics clinic (monthly leads 150 → 1,500, 10×; two Google-penalized sites recovered) and a New York B2B tech company (marketing-influenced bookings $150K → $820K in two years, 5×; $300K+ influenced pipeline a month). The offer still needs strong risk reversal and founding-partner positioning.
- **Pricing model:** Setup fee + monthly retainer + performance bonus
- **Client minimum:** $3,000/month ad spend
- **12-month goal:** 10 clients ≈ $40–50k/month (≈ $4–5k average per client)
